The problem it solves

Same facts. Different decisions. And nobody knows why.

People with different decision-making sequences can look at the same facts and reach different decisions. Without knowing why, they read the difference as a difference in values, understanding or feeling, both consciously and subconsciously.

1

The disagreement

Two partners, or a leader and a team, disagree on the same facts.

2

The misreading

Each assumes the other does not care, does not understand, is not listening or is not committed.

3

The switch

Under stress, one or both switch to the sequence they do not normally use, and become harder to read.

4

The cost

Decisions stall, trust falls, and the conflict feels personal when it is about how each person decides.

What people problems cost

65%of high-potential start-ups fail because of conflict between the people who started them. Noam Wasserman, Harvard Business School, about 10,000 companies; reported by CNN Money, 2014.
23%of failed start-ups cited "not the right team", and 13% cited disharmony among the team or investors. CB Insights, 101 start-up post-mortems; reported by LaunchRock.

These studies did not measure decision-making sequences. The link between the two is Brandscaling's hypothesis.

How you decide

Two sequences. Every decision runs through one of them.

Every decision you make uses both logic and feeling. What differs from person to person is where a decision starts, the sequence in between and what finally settles it. We call that your Dominant Decision Intelligence Sequence. Once you know yours, your partner’s and your team’s, the arguments that felt personal turn out to be two sequences reading the same facts differently. Clashes get resolved, everyone’s decision making improves, and the business runs with less friction.

What is happening in your body

The Human Decision Intelligence Network Mechanism

Decision Intelligence is understanding the network your body uses, consciously and subconsciously, to make a decision. Brandscaling's model shows how the brain, heart and gut work together when you decide, and what internal and external influences affect the decision-making process. It keeps two kinds of content visibly separate: verified anatomy, with sources, and the Brandscaling hypothesis.

Three centres

  • Brain, about 86 billion neurons: thinking, language, decisions.
  • Heart, about 40,000 neurons: sends body signals the brain reads as feelings. Gives off an electromagnetic field that one study detected in another person's brain recording, within about 3 ft.
  • Gut, 200 to 600 million neurons: its own nervous system.

What is verified

  • The vagus nerve links the brain, heart and gut, and 80 to 90% of its fibres carry signals from the body up to the brain. The brain receives more from the heart and gut than it sends to them.
  • The heart gives off a measurable electromagnetic field. One study detected one person's heart signal in another person's brain recording, within about 3 ft and strongest at touch.

The Brandscaling Hypothesis

  1. A feeling is created in your innate disposition.
  2. It is interpreted in the heart as a feeling.
  3. It is put into words in the brain as a thought.

The innate disposition mediates between the conscious and the subconscious. This part is Brandscaling's hypothesis.

How you learn

How you take in and process information: reading, listening, seeing or doing; step by step or big picture first; alone or with others; and at what pace. It sets how your reports, workbooks and AI mentors explain things to you, whether that is long detailed text, bullet points, images, videos or diagrams. We cover it all.

Part 5Neurodiversity & Accessibility

What environment you need to work well

The conditions under which your body performs consistently: focus, energy, sensitivity to noise and busy settings, how you respond to pressure, and the effect of what you eat and drink. Every person processes information in their own way. Your report shows you what affects you the most, to allow you to make better choices. Part 5 is not a diagnosis.

Part 6Mindset & Personality

Your mindset

The attitude and tone you bring into a decision before your sequence starts, throughout it and at the end: growth or fixed, confidence or self-doubt, patience or impulsiveness. Your report shows you how your mindset influences your outcomes, and the workbook helps you strengthen the areas you choose to work on.

Part 7Meta-Beliefs & Values

Your beliefs and values

The beliefs and values that allow, limit or override your decisions, even when logic and feeling agree. They explain why a decision can stall after it is made. Your report highlights your beliefs and values and how they affect your decision-making abilities. Part 7 does not judge whether a belief is right.

The Decision Frame

Bring any decision.
Work through it in your own sequence.

The Decision Frame in the app allows you to work through your own decision-making process, making sure you use the dominant sequence that is best for you. It puts your sequence on the screen as boxes. You fill each box in the order your sequence runs; the AI mentor guides you through the entire process, box by box.

The frame for an Architect

Your decisionWhat is the decision you are trying to make?
1 · LogicThe data, facts, numbers and plan you already have
2 · FeelingHow it makes you feel, and how the people involved react+ add another
3 · LogicThe new data that answers box 2+ add another
4 · Your decisionWritten out, with the logic and data it rests on

Boxes 2 and 3 together are the sequence repeating. They can carry on for as long as the decision needs. If box 4 holds a feeling instead of logic, the mentor names the gap: your sequence settles with logic, so which data supports it?

The frame for an Alchemist

Your decisionWhat is the decision you are trying to make?
1 · FeelingHow it feels, the sense of rightness, the story
2 · LogicWhether the numbers and facts support it+ add another
3 · FeelingWhat the data does to the feeling+ add another
4 · Your decisionWritten out, with the feeling and story it rests on

If box 4 rests on numbers that box 3 says still do not feel right, the mentor names the gap: your sequence settles with feeling, so what would make the story right, or which constraint are you holding instead?

It never blocks you

Every warning names the box and the gap. No warning stops you deciding.

It works in your order

It does not ask an Architect how something feels before the data, and does not ask an Alchemist for numbers before the story.

It remembers

Each decision is logged: the date, the order used, any warnings, and what you decided. Under pressure, it reminds you of your stress result before box 4.
